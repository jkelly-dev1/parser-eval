SOURCE DOCUMENTS AND WHY EACH ONE MAY BE SHIPPED
=================================================
Every document in this folder must be redistributable, because the corpus
ships with the code. A benchmark whose documents exist only as URLs stops
being reproducible the moment a link moves.

The bar is a POSITIVE statement of rights, not an absence of a restriction.
"I could not find a prohibition" is what disqualified the first corpus.

1901_Census1900_Vol1Pt2_Census-Bureau.pdf
-----------------------------------------
  What      Twelfth Census of the United States, taken in 1900. Volume 1,
            Population, Part 2. Published 1901. 94 pages, scanned.
  Source    US Census Bureau
            https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/1900/
            volume-1/volume-1-p2.pdf
  Rights    Covered twice over, which is why it was chosen:
            (a) PUBLISHED 1901. Anything published in the United States
                before 1930 is in the public domain by age, regardless of
                anything else.
            (b) A work of the Census Office, a US federal agency. Works of
                the US government prepared by officers or employees as part
                of their official duties are not subject to copyright.
            Neither argument depends on a website's terms page remaining
            online -- both of the pages consulted for other candidates
            returned 404 while this was being checked.
  Not used  No page from this document is in the corpus, and it is KEPT ON
            ITS MERITS: it tests a case no page in the corpus can ask, a text
            layer that is present and lying rather than absent or correct.
            Cutting it is the obvious next extension.
  Why it is here
            PDF page 40 (printed lvi) is the hardest page found so far: TWO
            independent seven-column tables side by side, 1900 type, foxing
            and scan noise, ruled columns, dotted leaders, superscript
            footnote markers.
            It carries PER-ROW ARITHMETIC -- 1900 population minus 1890
            population equals the printed increase, and that increase over
            the 1890 figure gives the printed percentage. Amherst county:
            17,864 - 17,551 = 313, and 313/17,551 = 1.8%.
            It also has a property no document in the first corpus had: ITS
            EMBEDDED TEXT LAYER IS OCR OUTPUT AND THE OCR IS WRONG. The page
            header extracts as "l'Ol'Ul,ATl(JN IN" and a county name as
            "Amhtor.t". A parser that trusts the text layer inherits those
            errors and reports them with confidence.


RG29-2790961-2926-1929-504-000{25,29,34,37}.jpg
-----------------------------------------------
  What      Four sheets from a 1929 Census of Manufactures schedule, FILLED
            IN BY HAND. Grayscale scans, about 1,820 x 2,420 pixels each. The
            filename is the National Archives' own: record group 29 (Records
            of the Bureau of the Census), followed by the series and item
            identifiers and the sheet number.
  Source    US National Archives and Records Administration, catalog record
            50607213.
  Rights    Federal records of the Bureau of the Census, created by census
            officers in the course of their official duties, and so not
            subject to copyright on the same basis as the two Census Bureau
            and two FDIC publications here. NARA's catalog record states both
            the USE RESTRICTION and the ACCESS RESTRICTION as "Unrestricted".
            That is the positive statement this folder requires, and it comes
            from the holding institution rather than from an inference.
  Privacy   RECORDED RATHER THAN GLOSSED, because handwriting is the one case
            where the rights question and the privacy question come apart.
            These are schedules about BUSINESSES -- employees, payroll,
            materials, products, fuel, sales -- not about households or named
            individuals, which is what disqualified every other handwriting
            source considered. They do carry the handwriting and, on sheet 3,
            the signatures of the officers who completed the form in 1929.
            The records are 97 years old, they are federal records, and NARA
            has released them without an access or use restriction. There is
            no living-individual data here in any ordinary sense, and no
            personal financial or household information at all.
  Used      00025 is corpus page census29_wages. 00034 is census29_sales.
            00029 and 00037 were downloaded from the same series as
            candidates and are NOT cut into the corpus; they are 2 MB each
            and nothing in the repository references them.
  Why it is here
            THESE ARE THE ONLY PAGES IN THE CORPUS WITH NO TEXT LAYER AT ALL,
            and the only ones that separate the tool families instead of
            ranking them. Every other page has something a parser can copy;
            on these, a tool either reads pixels or returns nothing. The
            control that returns the embedded text layer scores 0.0% on both,
            which is what a floor looks like.

            00025, sheet 2. Employees, salaries, materials and products.
            Three handwritten sums check against the hand labels:
              10,951 + 60 + 300      = 11,311
              3,120 + 6,630 + 14,500 = 24,250
              1 + 2                  = 3 salaried men
            Struck-through corrections, marginal annotations in a second and
            third hand, and a noisy skewed grayscale scan.

            00034, sheet 3. The same schedule, sparsely filled: power
            equipment, fuel, distribution of sales. 35,000 + 2,000 = 37,000
            checks against the handwritten total. Two signatures, and
            "x x x x x" fill patterns in the cells that do not apply --
            a mark meaning "none" that is not a value and must not be read
            as one.


fdic-ar-1956.pdf
-----------------
  What      Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, Annual Report 1956.
            176 pages, scanned, with an ABBYY FineReader OCR text layer.
  Source    Obtained via https://www.fdic.gov/history/1900-1919
            The scan itself is FRASER's: the page footers read "Digitized for
            FRASER / http://fraser.stlouisfed.org/ / Federal Reserve Bank of
            St. Louis".
  Rights    The FDIC is a US federal agency, so its annual report is a work
            prepared by federal officers and employees as part of their
            official duties and is not subject to copyright. The audit letter
            inside it is signed by the Comptroller General -- GAO, also
            federal.
            The scan being FRASER's does not change that: a faithful
            photographic reproduction of a public-domain document does not
            attract a new copyright in the United States. FRASER's own rights
            page returned HTTP 403 when checked on 2026-08-16, so no statement
            from them is quoted here -- the basis is the underlying document,
            not the scanner.
            ONE EXCEPTION IS WORTH STATING RATHER THAN ASSUMING: a
            government publication can REPRINT third-party copyrighted
            material, and federal-works status does not extend to what it
            reprints. The three pages graded here carry none -- they are FDIC
            and GAO financial statements throughout. A page chosen later that reproduces an outside chart
            or photograph would need its own look.
  Used      Pages 36, 37 and 144, as corpus pages fdic_balance, fdic_income
            and fdic_earnings.
  Why it is here
            THE ARITHMETIC IS EXACT AND THERE IS A LOT OF IT, which is what
            makes hand labels verifiable rather than merely careful.

            page 36, balance sheet at 30 June 1956. Nested three deep:
              1,810,140,116 + 6,707,097               = 1,816,847,213
              five receivership items                 = 11,277,003
              11,277,003 - 8,346,249                  = 2,930,754
              five asset lines                        = 1,822,462,212
              six liability lines                     = 131,643,818
              131,643,818 + 1,690,818,394             = 1,822,462,212
            THE SHEET BALANCES: assets equal liabilities plus fund, exactly.

            page 37, statement of income. Chains further, and its closing fund
            figure 1,690,818,394 is the same one that appears on page 36 -- a
            CROSS-PAGE invariant, which nothing in the first corpus had.

            page 144, Table 114, earnings and expenses by state. TWO-
            DIMENSIONAL: total rows sum their sub-rows, AND the US column
            equals Continental US plus Alaska, Puerto Rico and Other on every
            row (7,211,080 + 6,658 + 11,759 + 2,424 = 7,231,921). A misread
            digit breaks either its row or its column.

            The pages are hard in the ways that matter: dotted leaders, three
            indent levels encoding hierarchy, values landing in different
            columns by level, footnote references inside labels, a line item
            whose value is the single digit 1, and numbers embedded in label
            prose ("face value, $1,815,200,000") that are NOT column values.
            Page 144 is additionally rotated 90 degrees on a portrait page
            with eleven numeric columns.
  Note      IT IS A SCAN CARRYING AN ACCURATE OCR LAYER -- one full-page JBIG2
            image per page with ABBYY text over it. Spot-checked figures in
            the layer are correct. So a parser reading the text layer inherits
            ABBYY's work while one reading the pixels does its own, and those
            are different measurements.
            SEPARATING THEM IS WHY THE CORPUS HAS A CONTROL: an adapter that
            returns the embedded text layer and does no work at all. On these
            three pages it beats several real parsers, which means those tools
            were relaying ABBYY rather than reading the page. build_scan.py
            attacks the same question from the other side, by re-rendering
            pages into image-only PDFs so that nothing but the pixels remains.


fdic-ar-2023.pdf
-----------------
  What      Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, Annual Report 2023.
            250 pages, BORN-DIGITAL. Typeset in Adobe InDesign 19.2 and
            written out by Adobe PDF Library 17.0; the PDF is tagged and its
            text layer is the publisher's own, not anyone's OCR.
  Source    https://www.fdic.gov/about/financial-reports/reports/
            2023annualreport/2023-arfinal.pdf
  Rights    A work of the United States Government, on the same basis as the
            1956 report above, and the byline supports it rather than merely
            permitting it: the PDF's own metadata names the author as
            "FDIC - Division of Finance/Jannie Eaddy". That is an agency
            division and a named employee, NOT a contractor -- the test that
            disqualified the HUD document listed under CONSIDERED AND NOT
            INCLUDED below.

            The FDIC states the position itself: "Information that is created
            by or for the US government on this site is within the public
            domain. Public domain information on the FDIC Archive may be
            freely distributed and copied. However, it is requested that in
            any subsequent use of this work, FDIC be given appropriate
            acknowledgment." THAT ACKNOWLEDGMENT IS GIVEN HERE AND IN THE
            README, because it was asked for.

            READ HONESTLY, THE PROVENANCE OF THAT QUOTE HAS A WRINKLE AND IT
            IS RECORDED RATHER THAN SMOOTHED OVER. The statement is on
            archive.fdic.gov, which returns 403 to automated requests; it was
            read from the Internet Archive's capture of that page, not from
            the live page, and the report itself was downloaded from
            www.fdic.gov. The primary basis for shipping it is therefore the
            statutory one -- a work of the US Government is not subject to
            copyright -- with the agency's own policy as corroboration. An
            operator who wants the live page confirmed should open it in a
            browser, where it serves normally.

            No copyright notice, credit line or third-party photo attribution
            appears anywhere in the 250 pages.
  Used      Page 134 (printed 132), as corpus page fdic2023_balance.
  Why it is here
            IT IS THE ONLY BORN-DIGITAL PAGE IN THE CORPUS, and it exists to
            answer a question the scans cannot. Three pages here are scans
            carrying somebody else's OCR, and on those the strongest
            "parser" in the comparison turns out to be the embedded text
            layer doing nothing at all. That result is only interpretable
            against a page whose text layer was typeset rather than guessed.

            IT IS ALSO THE SAME PUBLICATION AS fdic-ar-1956.pdf, 67 YEARS
            LATER. The 1956 balance sheet and the 2023 balance sheet are the
            same statement of the same fund, one a photograph of paper and
            one never scanned at all. That is as close to a controlled
            comparison as a corpus of real documents is going to get.

            Page 134 (printed 132) is the Deposit Insurance Fund balance
            sheet: TWO YEAR COLUMNS, so a parser must keep 2023 and 2022
            apart; NEGATIVE FIGURES PRINTED IN PARENTHESES, which is a sign
            and not punctuation; four figures buried in italic parentheses
            inside row labels; and twelve sums that close to the dollar in
            both years, with the sheet balancing in each.


CONSIDERED AND NOT INCLUDED
---------------------------
Review_Of_Structure_Materials.pdf, US Department of Housing and Urban
Development. NOT INCLUDED because it was prepared under contract by NAHB
Research Center, Inc. A contractor is not a government employee, the report's
own disclaimer distances it from the US Government, and it credits a
third-party cover photograph. CHECK THE BYLINE, NOT THE DOMAIN: a .gov URL is
not a statement of rights, and this is the document that proves it.

Federal Reserve statistical release H.4.1. A good born-digital dense table
with real internal arithmetic. NOT INCLUDED because the Board's copyright and
legal-notices pages both returned 404 and no positive statement of rights
could be read from the publisher. The federal-works rule probably covers it;
"probably" is not the bar.

NIST Special Database 19, the obvious handwriting dataset. Freely
downloadable, but the page carries no license language and NIST's own guidance
is to contact them for terms.


THE HANDWRITTEN PAGE, AND HOW IT WAS RESOLVED
---------------------------------------------

The single most valuable page in the first corpus was a hand-filled form,
because it was the only one that separated the tool families completely --
zero values recovered by the conventional parsers against most of the page by
a VLM. A corpus without one collapses into "everything works".

THE OBSTACLE WAS NEVER COPYRIGHT, IT WAS PRIVACY. The handwriting sources that
are easy to find are genuine records carrying a private individual's hand:
census population schedules, draft cards, ship manifests. Public domain, and
still somebody's personal data. The fallback proposal was to MANUFACTURE one
-- print a public-domain form, fill it in by hand, photocopy it crooked, scan
it -- which would have been unambiguously ours to ship at the cost of making
the difficulty artificial.

THAT WAS NOT NEEDED. The 1929 Census of Manufactures schedules above are the
resolution: real records, real pencil, real corrections and marginalia, and
they are about BUSINESSES rather than people, released by NARA with neither an
access nor a use restriction. The corpus gets genuine handwriting without
publishing anyone's private information, and the difficulty is the document's
own rather than something staged.
